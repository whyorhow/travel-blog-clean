# Nomad Scribbles — Editorial System

Observation-first writing model. Record what was seen, moved through, and physically experienced — not what a place "is".

---

## 1. Core editorial rule

Priority order:

1. Observation (what is physically present)
2. Action (what happened / what was done)
3. Detail (specific sensory information)
4. Only then reflection (if necessary)

Never lead with interpretation.

Never summarise the meaning of a place before showing it.

Avoid abstract framing unless it is grounded in a specific moment.

---

## 2. Forbidden language patterns (soft block)

Do NOT use unless absolutely necessary:

- introduce itself
- reveals itself
- without warning
- life continues
- in the spaces between
- layers / more than one layer
- constant reference
- existing in the same space
- hidden gem
- heartbeat / rhythm / story of the city
- without needing to compete
- the city feels like...
- the city is...

If used, it must be immediately followed by a concrete physical example.

---

## 3. Anti-genericity rule

A sentence is too generic if it could describe multiple cities.

If the sentence could apply to Athens, Rome, Prague, Vienna, Budapest, or Barcelona without change → rewrite it.

Replace abstraction with specificity:

- ❌ The city reveals itself slowly
- ✔ It took three walks before we noticed the small chapel behind the market

- ❌ Life continues beneath ancient streets
- ✔ A café opened its shutters below cracked stone columns while commuters passed without looking up

---

## 4. Observation test (mandatory before publishing)

For every paragraph, ask:

- Could I photograph this sentence?
- Does it contain a physical object, place, or action?
- Is there at least one specific detail (not general atmosphere)?
- Am I describing or interpreting?

If interpretation > observation → rewrite.

---

## 5. Section behaviour rules

**First Impressions**

- Must be purely observational
- No meaning, no summary
- Focus on arrival-level detail (scale, noise, movement, layout)

**Closer Look**

- Movement through space
- 2–4 specific visual details
- At least 1 human moment (people, behaviour, interaction)

**Reflective close**

- Must be memory-based, not summary-based
- One moment that stayed
- One sensory detail (light / sound / temperature)
- No explanation of "what the city means"

---

## 6. Personification limit

Avoid assigning intention to places:

- ❌ the city invites / expects / speaks / reveals / hides

Prefer:

- ✔ we noticed / we saw / it appeared / it shifted / it changed as we moved

Cities do not act. They are observed.

---

## 7. Specificity requirement

Every page must contain:

- named places (real, specific)
- at least 3 physical details per section
- at least 1 moment of human behaviour per page

If a paragraph contains only abstract description → it is incomplete.

---

## 8. Replacement loop (when editing)

When rewriting, always convert:

abstract idea → physical observation

Examples:

- "layers of history" → stone columns beside a bus stop
- "hidden spaces" → courtyard visible only through a narrow gate
- "the city feels alive" → vendors lifting metal shutters at 7am

---

## 9. Nomad Scribbles style rule

Write as if:

- you are remembering a place, not analysing it
- the reader will never visit
- every detail must carry visual weight

Avoid explaining what matters. Let repetition of observation create meaning naturally.
